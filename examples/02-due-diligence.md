# Due diligence call transcript

Marcus: OK team, we have an hour for Target Co. Priya, walk us through the deal one more time before we go to vote.

Priya: Target Co is a B2B SaaS platform serving mid-market construction firms. They've been around four years, currently at $8M ARR, growing 140% year-over-year. Founders are both ex-Procore, so they know the vertical cold.

Tom: I had dinner with the CEO last week. He's impressive — clear thinker, deeply technical, and surprisingly humble for a founder coming off a big growth quarter.

Lin: Their product is in the top quartile on G2 for their category. Customer reviews are unusually positive — average 4.7 stars across 800+ reviews.

Marcus: What's the customer profile?

Priya: Mid-market construction firms, $50M-$500M annual revenue. Average contract is $80K ARR. Net revenue retention is 128% on the cohort that's been on platform 18+ months.

Tom: That NRR is exceptional for this segment. Construction software typically caps at 110% NRR because the customer growth ceiling is real.

Marcus: I'm feeling good about this one. Strong team, strong product, strong category metrics. My instinct is we lead the round.

Priya: I agree. I think we should move fast — there's a competing term sheet from Andreessen.

Tom: Let's vote at the end of the call, but I'm leaning yes.

Marcus: OK so assuming we're leading at $200M post on $30M check, what's the path to a $1B exit?

Priya: The realistic exit path is acquisition by a vertical player — Autodesk, Trimble, or Procore. Construction tech has had 8 acquisitions in the $500M-$2B range in the past three years.

Tom: Or IPO if they hit $100M+ ARR. Construction tech doesn't have many public companies yet, so there'd be appetite.

Marcus: Either path looks reasonable.

Lin: I pulled the historical comparable transactions. The median multiple in this segment has been 15x ARR for the past 24 months.

Tom: That gives a $1.2B exit at $80M ARR. Probably realistic in 5-6 years given their growth rate.

Marcus: Good. Now let me actually look at the financial diligence. Priya, what does the P&L look like?

Priya: Revenue I covered. On the cost side — gross margin is 81%, which is in line with vertical SaaS. Operating expenses are 60% R&D, 25% S&M, 15% G&A.

Tom: That's a heavy R&D mix. They're not optimizing for short-term growth.

Marcus: Burn rate?

Priya: $1.2M per month current burn. They have 18 months runway at current burn, longer if they slow R&D hiring.

Lin: I dug into the customer cohort retention. The 2022 cohort is at 92% logo retention after 18 months, which is strong. But the 2023 cohort is at 88% — slight deceleration.

Tom: That's worth examining. Why the dip?

Lin: The 2023 cohort includes some smaller customers — they opened up to firms with $20M-$50M revenue, which churns more. If you exclude those, the 2023 cohort matches 2022.

Marcus: OK, segmentation issue rather than product issue.

Priya: I confirmed with the CEO. They've since tightened ICP to >$50M revenue customers.

Tom: What about the sales motion? Is it founder-led or do they have a sales team?

Priya: They have a 6-person sales team, founder still closes deals over $200K ARR. CAC payback is 14 months, which is decent.

Marcus: Have we checked references on the team?

Lin: Yes, three references on the CEO, two on the CTO. All universally positive. One reference flagged that the CEO can be slow on hiring decisions but called him "the best CEO I've worked with."

Tom: That matches my read.

Marcus: OK, I think the financial profile is in line with what I expected. Let's vote.

Priya: I'm a yes.

Tom: Yes from me.

Lin: I'm a yes.

Marcus: I'm a yes. We're leading the round. Priya, send the term sheet response by EOD.

Priya: On it.

Marcus: Great call everyone. Thanks for the rigor.
